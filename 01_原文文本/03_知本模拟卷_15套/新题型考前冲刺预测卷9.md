### Passage 2

Directions: Read the following passage. After reading it, you should give brief answers to the 5 questions (No.16 to No.20) that follow. The answers (in no more than 3 words) should be written after the corresponding numbers on the Answer Sheet.

I do a lot of management training each year for the Circle K Corporation, which is a national chain of convenience stores（便利店）. Among the topics we discuss in our seminars（研讨会）is how to keep good employees working at our company—a real challenge to managers when you consider the low pay in the service industry. During these discussions, I ask the participants, "What has caused you to stay long enough to become a manager?" After a while a new manager, Cynthia, took up the question, and slowly, with her voice almost breaking, said, "It was a $19 baseball glove（手套）."

Cynthia told the group that she had originally taken a Circle K clerk job as a temporary（临时的）position while she looked for something better. On her second or third day behind the counter, she received a phone call from her nine-year-old son, Jessie. He needed a baseball glove for Little League. She explained that as a single mother, money was very tight, and her first check would have to go for paying bills. Perhaps she could buy his baseball glove with her second or third check.

When Cynthia arrived for work the next morning, Patricia, the store manager, asked her to come to the small room in the back of the store that served as an office. Cynthia wondered if she had done something wrong or left some part of her job incomplete from the day before. She was upset and confused.

Patricia handed her a box. "I overheard you talking to your son yesterday," she said, "and I know

<!-- 第 4 页 -->

that it is hard to explain things to kids. This is a baseball glove for Jessie because he may not understand how important he is, even though you have to pay bills before you can buy gloves. You know we can't pay good people like you as much as we would like to; but we do care, and I want you to know you are important to us."

The thoughtfulness, passion and love of this convenience store manager demonstrate vividly that people remember more how much an employer shows care and concern than how much the employer pays—an important lesson for the price of a Little League baseball glove.
